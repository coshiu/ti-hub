Rules of Engagement · 01

Cool Down Period

Timing matters. Respect prior engagement windows before re-initiating contact.

General rule
⏱ 3-Month Window

Do not re-engage candidates contacted within the past 3 months. Allow a full cool-down before outreach.

Interviewed candidates
🗓 1-Year Window

Avoid re-engaging candidates who have interviewed at Crusoe within the past year without explicit HM buy-in.

!Exceptions & Edge Cases
1
Business-need exception
▼

Exceptions can be made when eligibility requirements shift based on business needs. Use discretion and craft messaging that acknowledges the prior contact. Document the exception and rationale.

2
Re-engaging a prior interviewer
▼

Before reaching out to anyone who has interviewed at Crusoe recently, confirm that the hiring manager is genuinely interested in considering them for the new role. Don't assume interest — get explicit confirmation first.

Rules of Engagement · 02

Candidate History

Every candidate has context. Always check before you reach out.

Before any outreach: Check notes and recent activity in Ashby. This is non-negotiable — you may be reaching out to someone with a prior relationship with Crusoe.
→If a Candidate Has Previously Interviewed
Acknowledge it in your message
"I see you've been in touch with us before — I wanted to check in to see if now might be a good time to revisit opportunities at Crusoe."

This signals respect, professionalism, and awareness — and reflects well on Crusoe's brand.

Rules of Engagement · 03

Outreach Commitment

If we reach out, we owe a response. Always close the loop — even when circumstances change.

The Commitment

If we proactively contact a candidate and they respond with interest, we owe them a conversation — even if the role requirements have shifted.

→What This Means in Practice
  • Be transparent if the role has evolved or requirements have changed since initial outreach.
  • Close the loop with every interested candidate — silence after outreach damages Crusoe's reputation.
  • Respect their time: give them a real conversation, even a brief one, before archiving.
Candidate experience is a Crusoe brand signal. Every interaction — even a brief one — shapes how people talk about us in their networks.

Ashby Data Hygiene

Clean data powers better reporting. This is everyone's responsibility.

01Status Tracking
Required status states
ContactedInMail or message sent
InterestedPositive response received
Not InterestedDeclined or unresponsive
Logging requirements
  • Log InMails as they are sent
  • Update status throughout the process
  • Tag context for future follow-up
  • Add candidate detail notes for reporting accuracy
Why this matters: Accurate Ashby data is the foundation of sourcing reporting. Incomplete or stale status fields break pipeline analytics and make it harder to defend sourcing impact at the leadership level.
Best Practices

Core Sourcing Principles

The philosophy behind how we approach every search at Crusoe.

🎯
Calibrate Early, Calibrate Often
Confirm leveling, must-haves, and nice-to-haves upfront. Understand why the role matters, where prior candidates fell short, and what a strong fit looks like.
◈
Quality Over Quantity
Be intentional. A curated shortlist of well-qualified candidates is more impactful than mass outreach. Volume without signal creates noise, not pipelines.
🌱
Candidate Experience First
Every message is a reflection of Crusoe's brand — be clear, respectful, and human. Treat every candidate like they're deciding whether to work with us.
📍
Own Your Pipeline
Primary ownership matters. Stay close to your candidates, your HM relationships, and your domain. Depth over breadth creates consistent results.
The bar is high at Crusoe. A strong fit is someone who can speak the language of their domain and grow with the organization. Calibrate to that standard from the first search.
Best Practices

Communication Guidelines

Tone, personalization, and cadence — how we engage candidates at every stage.

01Tone & Personalization
Respect Seniority

Tailor tone and content based on the candidate's experience level and background. A Staff Engineer gets a different message than an L3.

Always Personalize

Lead with why them and why us. Generic messages signal low effort and reflect poorly on Crusoe. Personalization is not optional.

02Follow-Up Cadence
Initial outreach
Personalized first message — lead with why them, establish why Crusoe, clear ask.
Follow-up 1 · +3–5 business days
Light touch. Even a "no update" update keeps the candidate warm and informed.
Follow-up 2 · +3–5 business days
Add context, a new angle, or a specific Crusoe signal that may resonate.
Follow-up 3–4 · +3–5 business days each
Final attempts. Maintain tone — never pressure, always leave the door open gracefully.
Don't leave candidates in the dark. If there's no new information, a brief "still working on timing" message preserves the relationship and prevents drop-off.
Best Practices

Sourcing Credit & Handoffs

How credit is assigned, how handoffs work, and what happens after archive.

01Credit Assignment
First-touch ownership

The sourcer who first finds and engages a candidate keeps sourcing credit — even if that candidate is later moved to a different team, role, or process.

Credit duration

Credit remains with the original sourcer for as long as the candidate is active in the ATS. Once archived for 3+ months, the candidate becomes fair game for new sourcing — unless an exception applies.

02Candidate Handoffs
Step 01
POC introduces candidate
Current recruiter or sourcer introduces the candidate directly to the new team's recruiter.
→
Step 02
New recruiter takes ownership
After the intro, the new recruiter owns scheduling and interviewing from that point forward.
→
Step 03
Original sourcer steps back
No need for the original sourcer to stay involved post-handoff. Credit stays put.
Clean handoffs protect candidate experience. A warm intro with context is always better than a cold transfer — it signals continuity and respect to the candidate.
